# Brix Benchmark Protocol — Version 0.1

## Purpose and status

Use the ordered Proster 0–80% handheld refractometer to turn soluble-solids concentration into a practical, repeatable development benchmark for each finished preparation and, where useful, the finished drink.

- **Evidence class:** ORIGINAL EXPERIMENTAL METHOD informed by instrument-manufacturer guidance (`SRC-2026-091`)
- **Status:** READY WHEN EQ-002 IS RECEIVED, INSPECTED AND CALIBRATED
- **Numerical target:** NOT YET SET
- **Shelf-life meaning:** NONE

This method does not invent a target Brix. The first measurements establish an achievable reference from the relevant commercial control and the first source-traced batch. A later target window may be set only after flavour, texture and service function have passed.

## What the reading means

| Matrix | Reported term | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Clear sugar syrup or filtered cordial | Measured Brix, if the boundary is readable | Practical dissolved-solids/process-consistency reading; not a complete sugar analysis |
| Fruit or pumpkin purée/preparation | Apparent Brix | Includes the effect of soluble fruit/pumpkin solids and may be affected by pulp or an indistinct boundary |
| Nut/seed paste, dairy drink or emulsion | Apparent refractometer reading | Matrix interference may be substantial; use only for like-for-like process consistency if repeatable |
| Formula using tagatose, polyols or high-intensity sweeteners | Apparent Brix | Never treat equal Brix as equal sucrose, sweetness or solids without an ingredient-specific correction |

## Measurement sequence

1. Record instrument identity, serial number if present, manual/version, stated temperature-compensation range and smallest readable scale division.
2. Inspect and calibrate the instrument exactly as its own manual directs. Record calibration liquid, temperature, reading before adjustment and reading after adjustment.
3. Homogenise the complete sample using its recorded service or batch method. Avoid incorporating air. Record any settling, separation, filtration or remixing.
4. Bring the sample into the temperature range required by the instrument manual and record actual sample temperature.
5. Clean and dry the prism, apply enough sample to cover it uniformly, close the daylight plate and allow the reading to stabilise as the manual directs.
6. Record the raw reading and whether the boundary is sharp, slightly diffuse or unreadable. Do not estimate a value from an unreadable boundary.
7. Clean and dry the prism and repeat with a fresh aliquot. Continue only as needed to understand repeatability; do not discard inconvenient readings. Record all values and the observed range.
8. Report the result using the matrix-specific term above and retain the raw readings, temperature and boundary quality.

No repeatability tolerance is declared until the physical Proster manual and first instrument checks provide a basis.

## Comparative benchmark design

For each product and format, measure in the same session where practical:

1. the unmodified ingredient/input;
2. the closest like-for-like commercial control;
3. the source-traced house batch;
4. the finished drink only when both products are used in the same fixed drink build.

Record each raw reading and the arithmetic difference between the house sample and commercial control. Do not call one superior because its Brix is higher or closer. Sweetness, flavour clarity, cling/dispersion and service performance remain separate decisions.

If no like-for-like commercial control exists, the first successful source-traced batch becomes a **process baseline**, not a commercial benchmark. It may support later batch-consistency control but cannot create a pass range by itself.

## When a final target may be set

A dated product-specific Brix or apparent-Brix target window may be proposed only after:

- at least one source-traced formulation passes the relevant flavour, texture and service-function evaluation;
- the commercial control and successful house batch have both been measured using this protocol;
- the reading is repeatable enough on the actual matrix to be operationally useful; and
- the target is tied to observed successful samples rather than a generic syrup number.

The chosen target must name the product, format, instrument, sample temperature, matrix label, supporting batch/control IDs and data used. It is a consistency benchmark only. It must not be presented as proof of sugar content, healthfulness, preservation, microbial safety or shelf life.
