## Objective

Develop two source-traced pistachio formats: a real-pistachio preparation for matcha and other milk-led drinks, and a pourable syrup for faster café service and broader coffee, tea, zero-proof and cocktail use.

## Current evidence

- `SRC-2026-074`: Viani 95 g paste made from 100% roasted pistachios is the preferred low-commitment first input.
- `SRC-2026-084`: METRO Chef peeled pistachio kernels, 1 kg, are a later whole-nut comparison; availability and price require rechecking.
- `SRC-2026-073`: MONIN Pistachio 0.7 L is the first commercial syrup control.
- `SRC-2026-075`: 1883 Pistachio Syrup provides an additional commercial benchmark.
- `PR-0023` / `SRC-2026-076`: 1883 Pistachio & Chaï supplies an exact quantified professional application, not a reproducible syrup formula.

## Source and process gates

- No complete professional formula has yet been selected for a house pistachio paste preparation or house pistachio syrup.
- Do not dilute, sweeten, emulsify or acidify the purchased paste until a complete source lineage is selected.
- Do not reverse-engineer a commercial ingredient declaration.
- Whole-kernel processing is deferred until the paste-led direction passes and the café has demonstrated suitable grinding capability.
- The first operational batch must use practical gram quantities and explicitly document every departure from its source.

## Functional questions

- Does real pistachio remain identifiable beside matcha rather than reading only as sweetness or generic marzipan?
- Can a paste preparation disperse without an oily ring, coarse particles or excessive residue?
- Does the syrup outperform the paste in tea, coffee and cocktails while losing acceptable real-nut character?
- Is the premium input cost justified by measured dose and customer preference?

## Stage decision

Proceed with source selection and procurement comparison only. Buy no bulk kernels specifically for this project yet. No batch, application test, shelf-life claim or production approval is open.
