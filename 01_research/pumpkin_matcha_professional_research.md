## Research question

Which professional pumpkin preparations and texture systems best inform a sauce that tastes clearly of pumpkin, supports matcha, can be squeezed decoratively onto the inside wall of a cold glass, and remains there through a practical café build?

Flavour design and wall-cling structure are treated as separate questions. A recipe earns consideration only for the functions it actually demonstrates.

## Commercial café-sauce benchmarks

### Torani Puremade Pumpkin Pie Sauce — SRC-2026-021

The direct manufacturer ingredient list places cane sugar, water, and tapioca syrup first, followed by nonfat dry milk; xanthan and gellan gum appear later. The page names coffee, espresso/latte, chocolate, ice-cream, milkshake, and frappe uses. Pumpkin is not separately named in the ingredient declaration, although the product is sold on pumpkin-pie flavour; “natural flavors” remain unspecified.

- **SOURCE-STATED FACT:** This is a multi-application commercial beverage sauce using syrup solids, dairy solids, xanthan, and gellan.
- **EDITORIAL INTERPRETATION:** It is a useful texture/service control, but not evidence for clear real-pumpkin character.
- **UNKNOWN:** Formula quantities, physical properties, and wall-cling performance.

### Starbucks Pumpkin Spice Sauce in iced service — SRC-2026-025

Starbucks' current ingredient declaration for an iced pumpkin cream espresso lists its sauce as sugar, condensed skim milk, pumpkin puree, colour ingredients, natural flavours, annatto, salt, and potassium sorbate. It is used inside cold foam, not as a glass decoration.

- **SOURCE-STATED FACT:** A real-pumpkin, condensed-milk sauce has direct cold café service precedent.
- **EDITORIAL INTERPRETATION:** The architecture reinforces condensed milk as a credible body/sweetness direction, but formula and cling remain unknown.

## Texture evidence

### Low-shear resistance and service flow

Heyman et al. (`SRC-2026-023`) found that guar, xanthan, and CMC increased consistency index and yield stress in their commercial béchamel model; all reduced syneresis, with xanthan most effective in that specific study. This supports testing texture as a controlled variable.

- **HYPOTHESIS:** A preparation with sufficient structure at rest but lower resistance during squeeze/shear could satisfy both wall retention and bottle dispensing.
- **LIMITATION:** The study matrix and tested levels cannot be copied into sweet pumpkin sauce as if performance were guaranteed.

### Low-cost comparative measurement

The USDA Bostwick method (`SRC-2026-022`) demonstrates the value of fixed temperature, fixed elapsed time, distance travelled, remixing, repeats, and agreement rules. PB-001's vertical cold-glass method applies those control principles but is an `ORIGINAL EXPERIMENTAL METHOD`, not a Bostwick result. See `../04_round_1_development/pumpkin_matcha/glass_cling_test_protocol.md`.

## Shelf-life boundary

Source-stated storage ranges for PR-0013 and PR-0014 are preserved as source claims only. They do not transfer to an adapted or original formula. FDA guidance (`SRC-2026-024`) makes clear, within its shelf-stable commercial scope, that pH and water activity are joint classification variables and that process/container matter. A pH reading alone cannot approve PB-001 shelf life.

PB-001 therefore remains a refrigerated development preparation with shelf life `TBD`; a conservative discard plan and local food-service requirements must be set before Batch 001.

## Initial selection rationale

No sourced recipe deserves direct adoption. The strongest laboratory direction is a real-pumpkin sauce whose flavour base and texture system can be varied independently, compared against a condensed-milk professional benchmark and a commercial sauce control. This is provisionally a **sauce**, not a cordial: the defining requirement is decorative wall retention and squeeze service, while acidity and broad drink dilution are not yet primary functions.

Any later formulation must remain an exact sourced recipe or a declared adaptation of a professional/commercial source. It should not be written until the actual pumpkin input, dairy/vegan constraint, service bottle, benchmark behaviour, and a suitable source lineage are known.

## German procurement decision — 2026-08-30

### Default pumpkin input: KoRo Kürbispüree 425 g

KoRo's direct German page (`SRC-2026-026`) listed the 425 g product at EUR 3.25, immediately available with 2–4 working-day delivery, and containing 100% pumpkin. Its linked manufacturer specification (`SRC-2026-027`) describes a fine-grained, smooth but not pasty orange purée. Brix, pH, lot variability, and actual wall/squeeze performance remain unmeasured.

Libby's 425 g (`SRC-2026-028`) is retained as a recognised optional input comparator, but the checked German retailer displayed it as sold out at EUR 5.99. It should not delay V02. Fresh roasted pumpkin remains a later process/flavour comparison rather than the default café route.

### Commercial functional control: FONTE Gourmet Pumpkin Spice Sauce

FONTE's manufacturer page (`SRC-2026-029`) positions the 350 g squeeze bottle for professional cafés, hot/cold drinks, lattes, frappés, and toppings. A German coffee retailer listed it at EUR 19.95 with 7–14 working-day delivery (`SRC-2026-030`). This makes it a practical functional/service comparator.

Available narrative descriptions mention pumpkin, but the ingredient declaration captured by another European retailer (`SRC-2026-031`) names glucose syrup, condensed milk, sunflower oil, sugar, milk powder, molasses, emulsifier, natural flavours, sodium benzoate, and potassium sorbate—without separately named pumpkin. FONTE is therefore a **texture/squeeze/service control**, not evidence of real-pumpkin superiority. The purchased physical label must be photographed and may supersede online copy.

## Batch 001 selection

`B-20260830-PUMPMAT-V02-01` is a practical, approximately half-scale, weight-based adaptation of PR-0014 using KoRo purée and Nestlé Milchmädchen as German inputs. It retains the source ingredient set and one-minute cook, but replaces source volumes with deliberately rounded gram targets and adds controlled measurement/cooling. It is classified `ADAPTATION OF SOURCED RECIPE`, not an exact published recipe. The earlier V01 plan was superseded before execution because exact 25% scaling produced impractical bench quantities.

V02 contains **no texture modifier**. This is deliberate: first establish whether the strongest professional body precedent already meets the function. A xanthan/gellan/starch/other modified version, if justified by the comparative pilot, requires a new professional/commercial source lineage and a separately identified `ADAPTATION OF SOURCED RECIPE` batch.

## Research conclusion

Professional evidence supports pumpkin preparations as sauces and syrups across coffee, milk, and cocktail applications, but no verified source yet demonstrates the precise cold-glass decorative function. PB-001 should proceed through an objective benchmark pilot and controlled adaptations of professional/commercial source formulations, not by relabelling an existing pumpkin syrup as a cordial.
