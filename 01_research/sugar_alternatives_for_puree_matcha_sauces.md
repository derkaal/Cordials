# Sugar Alternatives for Purée Matcha Sauces — Initial Evidence Review

Date: 2026-08-31
Applies to: PB-001, PB-002, PB-003
Decision status: **RESEARCH COMPLETE FOR FIRST PILOT; NO FORMULA CHANGE AUTHORISED**

## Decision in one sentence

There is no serious candidate that can be promised to give exactly the same taste and service behaviour as sucrose while also being unambiguously “healthier”; keep granulated sucrose in the provenance-preserving first pumpkin pilot, then test pure D-tagatose as the leading lower-glycaemic, sugar-like **separate experimental comparison** if a suitable food-use product and café labelling position are confirmed.

## Why this is a formulation-system question

Sucrose supplies sweetness, dissolved solids, Brix, body, water binding, boiling behaviour, and some glass cling. High-intensity sweeteners provide sweetness but almost no mass. Polyols and alternative sugars provide mass but differ in sweetness, cooling, crystallisation, digestion, energy declaration, and regulation.

The pumpkin V02 also contains 190 g Nestlé Milchmädchen. The current product declaration states 55 g sugars per 100 g (`SRC-2026-059`), so the planned condensed milk contributes about 104.5 g sugars before counting the separate 50 g sucrose or the pumpkin. At the planned 18 g sauce dose, the pre-cook ingredient calculation attributes about 5.2 g sugars to the condensed milk and 2.5 g to the granulated sugar; actual concentration depends on measured cooked yield. Replacing only the 50 g granulated portion therefore does not create a low-sugar sauce and must not support a broad “healthy” claim.

## Evidence boundary

- WHO advises against using non-sugar sweeteners as a weight-control or chronic-disease prevention strategy; the recommendation is conditional and excludes polyols (`SRC-2026-048`).
- DGE's 2026 review says authorised sweeteners can reduce sugar and energy and have little effect on blood glucose, but the evidence on body weight, metabolism, and the microbiome is heterogeneous. DGE prioritises reducing the preference for sweetness over merely swapping sweeteners (`SRC-2026-049`).
- BfR lists the EU-authorised high-intensity sweeteners and polyols and notes that authorisation requires safety assessment; authorisation alone is not evidence of a health benefit (`SRC-2026-050`).
- WHO classifies honey, syrups, fruit juice, and fruit-juice concentrates as free-sugar sources. Agave, maple, coconut sugar, date syrup, honey, and similar alternatives are therefore not a defensible health upgrade merely because they sound natural (`SRC-2026-058`).

## Candidate assessment

| Candidate | Taste/bulk fit for these sauces | Health/evidence position | Operational and regulatory limits | First-pilot decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D-tagatose** | Closest serious candidate identified: near-sucrose sweetness, a mild sugar-like profile, and bulk. | EU rules permit a lower post-meal blood-glucose-rise claim when qualifying replacement conditions are met; this is not a blanket “healthier” claim (`SRC-2026-052`). EFSA estimated about 3 kcal/g for labelling while noting insufficient data for a more accurate value (`SRC-2026-060`). | EU-authorised as D-tagatose (`SRC-2026-055`), but German retail products are expensive and their food/supplement positioning varies (`SRC-2026-056`). The checked pure product declares 100 g sugars and 300 kcal per 100 g, so this is not a route to a “low sugar” label. It can brown differently, carries an excessive-consumption laxative warning on the checked product, and will change refractometer interpretation. | **LEADING SEPARATE COMPARISON after V02**, subject to exact product and regulatory/label review. |
| **Xylitol (E 967)** | Approximately sucrose-equivalent sweetness and bulk, but a cooling effect can conflict with pumpkin/dairy and delicate lychee. | Lower-energy polyol; a 2024 observational/mechanistic/intervention study linked xylitol exposure with platelet reactivity and cardiovascular-event risk and called for further safety study (`SRC-2026-057`). This does not establish population-level causality but defeats a simple health halo. | Polyol warning is required above the applicable threshold; gastrointestinal tolerance matters. Highly dangerous to dogs if mishandled. | **DO NOT PRIORITISE** for a café “healthier” story. |
| **Erythritol (E 968)** | About 0.7 times sucrose sweetness; strong cooling and crystallisation risk; cannot reproduce sucrose alone. | EFSA set an ADI of 0.5 g/kg body weight/day mainly to protect against laxative effects and concluded that available evidence did not establish a causal cardiovascular link (`SRC-2026-051`). | More mass is needed for equal sweetness; polyol warning/maximum-use rules and total customer exposure matter. | **NOT A SOLO REPLACEMENT**; only consider later in a blend if sensory need justifies it. |
| **Isomaltulose** | Clean, sucrose-like profile but only about half the sweetness, so equal sweetness requires much more material. | Can support a lower post-meal glucose-rise claim under conditions (`SRC-2026-052`), but it remains a caloric sugar rather than a low-sugar solution. | More mass changes solids, cost, and cling. | **REJECT FOR THIS PURPOSE**. |
| **Steviol glycosides, sucralose, acesulfame K, etc.** | Very high sweetness but essentially no bulk; blends are normally needed to reduce aftertaste (`SRC-2026-049`). | Safe within authorised conditions does not equal demonstrated long-term health benefit; WHO does not recommend NSS for weight-control/NCD prevention (`SRC-2026-048`). | Recipe must replace lost solids/body separately; authorised uses and maximum levels must be checked for the exact food category. | **NOT A DIRECT REPLACEMENT**. |
| **Allulose** | Technically attractive at about 70% sucrose sweetness with bulk and low energy. | No health conclusion is needed because it is not an operational option. | An EU novel-food application remains part of the Commission record (`SRC-2026-053`), while the current Union-list update record does not show an allulose authorisation (`SRC-2026-054`). Treat it as not available for German café formulation unless a qualified regulatory check confirms a later authorisation. | **DO NOT BUY OR USE** for the current project. |
| **Honey/agave/maple/date/coconut sugar** | Distinct flavours, still contribute sugars and can alter colour/viscosity. | WHO free-sugar guidance includes honey and syrups (`SRC-2026-058`). | “Natural” is not a valid health comparison. | **FLAVOUR OPTIONS ONLY**, not health substitutions. |

## Recommended experimental sequence

1. Run `B-20260830-PUMPMAT-V02-01` unchanged. It is an adaptation of PR-0014 and establishes the real-pumpkin flavour, service texture, and control comparison.
2. Do not optimise shelf life or sweetness before the V02 flavour/cling/application gate.
3. If V02 passes or nearly passes, identify a commercially active or professional sourced sweetener formulation and open a new, explicit adaptation for comparison. Preserve the source formula beside every change; do not create an original sweetener formula.
4. First serious candidate: pure food-use D-tagatose. Do not silently use a tagatose/galactose, tagatose/isomalt, inulin, polydextrose, or sucralose blend.
5. Compare blind against the sucrose V02 on pumpkin clarity, matcha compatibility, sweetness character, aftertaste, cooling, body, squeeze pattern, wall descent, integration, and customer preference. Use comparative judgments; do not invent a health or sensory pass number.
6. Treat refractometer readings from non-sucrose formulas as **apparent Brix** unless an ingredient-specific correction is available. Do not use equal Brix as proof of equal solids or sweetness.
7. A materially lower-sugar pumpkin sauce will eventually require redesign of the condensed-milk system as well as the 50 g granulated sugar. That work requires a new professional/commercial source lineage and a disclosed adaptation; it must not be improvised outside PR-0014.

For a defensible health direction, the strongest first move is a good-tasting product at a lower total sweetness/dose, not a one-for-one “health” substitution. The closest taste/bulk experiment is D-tagatose; the clearest lower-sugar development path is a later reformulation of both sucrose **and** sweetened condensed milk.

## Commercial-language guardrail

Do not call a candidate “healthy,” “healthier,” “diabetic-friendly,” “low sugar,” or “reduced sugar” from ingredient reputation or a single blood-glucose characteristic. Any café/menu or packaged-food claim requires calculation from the executed formula, the exact ingredient specifications, the applicable EU claim conditions, and a separate regulatory review.
