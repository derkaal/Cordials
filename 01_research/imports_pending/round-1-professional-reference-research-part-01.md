## Original research

### Yuzu

Yuzu was initially assessed as the strongest crossover candidate for matcha and cocktails.

The research stated that Japanese tea specialist Nio Tea publishes a Yuzu Matcha Soda using matcha, yuzu and soda, and that commercial Japanese café recipes also combine yuzu syrup and matcha.

On the cocktail side, yuzu cordial was described as established bartender territory. A formulation attributed to bartender **Miles Macquarrie of Kimball House** was subsequently selected as the strongest Round 1 Batch 001 reference.

The formulation was recorded as:

- 650 g yuzu juice
- 650 g sugar
- 75 g citrus peel
- 22 g citric acid
- stated approximate yield in the research: about 1 L

The method was recorded as:

“Combine yuzu juice, sugar and peel in a saucepan. Heat gently, stirring until the sugar dissolves. As soon as it reaches a bare simmer, remove from heat. Cool and strain. Then add the citric acid proportionally to the finished cordial.”

The earlier response then scaled the formulation to an approximately 500 ml test batch:

- 325 g yuzu juice
- 325 g white sugar
- 37.5 g lemon peel — or yuzu peel if available
- 11 g citric acid

This scaling was a CALCULATION rather than the original published formulation.

The research stated that Macquarrie uses approximately ¼ oz / 7.5 ml in a Coconut Yuzu Martini.

The original assessment was:

“Verdict: ★★★★★. This should be first.”

Later comparative assessment again selected:

**Yuzu Batch 001 — Kimball House reference**

Rationale recorded:

“Exact formulation, credible bartender, clear acid architecture, reproducible.”

The research subsequently proposed Yuzu as one of the three Wave A formulations to establish the laboratory method.

### Strawberry–Vanilla

Strawberry was initially characterised as probably the strongest commercial crossover candidate.

Professional matcha precedent mentioned included Tenzo and Pluck Tea. The professional cordial research focused primarily on two bartender preparations.

The first was attributed to **Brian Elder**, inspired by Attaboy and used for his Aquarelle cocktail. The research recorded that strawberries are covered with sugar and left overnight and that the resulting translucent strawberry liquid is strained.

The operational relevance was explicitly highlighted later:

“Brian Elder explicitly created his strawberry cordial at The Eddy because muddling strawberries created prep time and spent-fruit waste. His method is simply strawberries covered with sugar overnight, then the extracted red liquid strained off.”

The second and ultimately preferred Round 1 formulation was attributed to **Jason Kosmas and Dushan Zaric of Employees Only**, from *Speakeasy*.

The original professional recipe was recorded as:

- 4 lb / approximately 1.81 kg ripe strawberries
- 1 cup / approximately 200 g granulated sugar
- 1 cup / 240 ml water
- zest of ½ lemon
- 1 vanilla bean, split and scraped

Method recorded:

“Put everything into a saucepan over medium heat. Bring to a boil while stirring, reduce to low and simmer about 25 minutes. Cool. Strain.”

The research stated that Employees Only specifies:

- refrigerated storage
- 7 days

A quarter-scale test formulation was then calculated:

- 454 g strawberries
- 50 g sugar
- 60 ml water
- small strip/zest equivalent to ⅛ lemon
- ¼ vanilla bean

This quarter batch was explicitly a CALCULATED scaling rather than a separately sourced formulation.

The research also noted:

“This one is technically described by Employees Only as somewhere between a purée and syrup rather than a modern acidified cordial—which is worth being precise about.”

The original selection verdict was:

“Verdict: ★★★★★ for café use. Probably your easiest seller.”

Later comparative assessment selected:

**Strawberry–Vanilla Batch 001 — Employees Only / Speakeasy**

Rationale recorded:

“Exact published recipe, strong professional provenance, directly relevant vanilla bridge to matcha.”

The later research also stated that Employees Only remained preferable to Brian Elder’s version for Round 1 because vanilla and lemon zest made the preparation especially relevant to milk and matcha.

### Peach–Tea–Thyme

The principal professional reference was **Diageo Bar Academy’s Peach Cordial**.

The research recorded that the formulation first requires a base cordial.

Base cordial:

- 500 g white sugar
- 500 g filtered water
- 10 g citric acid
- 10 g malic acid
- 10 g tartaric acid
- 5 g Maldon sea salt

Method recorded:

“Stir until completely dissolved.”

Peach cordial:

- 250 g fresh peach, sliced
- 20 g cinnamon sticks
- 2 g black tea
- 3 g fresh thyme, stalks included
- 500 g base cordial

Method recorded:

“Vacuum seal everything and sous-vide at 60°C for 30 minutes. Fine-strain into a clean bottle and refrigerate.”

The research stated that Diageo gives the cordial a refrigerated shelf life of:

- one month

This was preserved in the research as a professional source-stated operational claim, not as independently validated food-safety evidence.

The research interpretation was:

“This is a seriously interesting recipe because the acid system isn't just citric. Malic acid gives that lingering apple/stone-fruit acidity, while tartaric contributes wine-like sharpness.”

The research also said:

“Matcha + peach + black tea + thyme makes sense; my concern is the 20 g cinnamon. So I'd test the professional recipe unchanged before deciding whether a café-specific version should reduce it.”

That proposed reduction was not performed and was clearly framed as a possible later adaptation.

Original verdict:

“Verdict: ★★★★½. Potentially the most sophisticated of the five.”

The formulation was later selected as:

**Peach–Tea–Thyme Batch 001 — Diageo Bar Academy**

Rationale recorded:

“Exact formulation, multi-acid base, sous-vide method, tea/herb complexity, explicit professional shelf-life statement.”

A second professional fruit-cordial formulation was also mentioned later from **The Sylvester via StarChefs**. It was described as a strawberry-and-peach cordial in which strawberries and peaches are macerated with sugar, adjusted to **40 Brix**, and then adjusted with measured citric, lactic and malic acids.

No complete ingredient quantities for that formulation were reproduced in this chat.

The research interpreted this as evidence that professional formulation can involve deliberate Brix and acid adjustment rather than simply “fruit + sugar + acid to taste.”

### Lychee

The principal Batch 001 reference was attributed to **Adam Fournier at Spago Beverly Hills** and described as a Salted Lychee Cordial used in Spago’s Lychee Rosé Martini.

The professional formulation was recorded as:

- 3¾ cups lychee purée — Perfect Purée was recommended
- 150 ml Giffard Lychi-Li
- 1 tbsp Maldon sea salt
- ¼ tsp citric acid

Method recorded:

“Blend on medium-high for 5 minutes. Bottle and refrigerate.”

The research stated that the source gives the refrigerated cordial a storage period of:

- up to three weeks

The research explicitly identified a major limitation:

“There is one complication for us: this cordial contains alcohol because of the Giffard.

For cocktails: no problem whatsoever.

For a café matcha served as non-alcoholic: obviously not.”

The earlier response proposed that the original Spago formulation should first be made exactly as written as a professional benchmark and that a separate non-alcoholic version might later be developed. No non-alcoholic replacement formulation was given because doing so without testing would have required inventing quantities.

The research noted that Spago uses approximately:

- ¾ oz / ~22 ml cordial

in a cocktail with reposado tequila, manzanilla, elderflower liqueur and lime.

Original verdict:

“Verdict: ★★★★★ flavour concept; ★★★☆☆ operationally because we need a second NA formulation.”

Separate professional evidence cited earlier included the US Bartenders’ Guild 2025 World Class material, in which an **acidified lychee cordial** was mentioned in Dalton’s *Shinhidaka*, alongside bourbon, sake, roasted shishito syrup and salted sakura.

Exact formulation quantities for that USBG lychee cordial were not reproduced in this chat.

Later comparative assessment selected:

**Lychee Batch 001 — Adam Fournier / Spago Beverly Hills**

Rationale recorded:

“Exact salted, acidified formulation; strong restaurant-bar provenance. Main issue: contains lychee liqueur, so it is not suitable as our final NA matcha formulation.”

### Pandan

Pandan was added to the Round 1 set after the original five-candidate discussion and eventually replaced passion fruit as one of the six experimental archetypes.

The later research concluded:

“Pandan is much stronger than I expected—Pacific Cocktail Haven and Birds of Paradise both use it as a true bar component, not merely dessert syrup.”

The principal Batch 001 reference was attributed to **Kevin Diedrich / Pacific Cocktail Haven**, in connection with the Leeward Negroni.

The exact formulation recorded in this chat was:

- 3 pandan leaves
- 4 oz Everclear
- infuse for 48 hours
- strain
- add 6 oz simple syrup

The research described it as:

“Exact formulation and excellent professional provenance; very simple extraction, though alcoholic.”

The alcohol content was therefore identified as a limitation for a universal café/matcha platform cordial.

A second professional pandan formulation was cited from **Birds of Paradise** via StarChefs and described as an **elote-pandan cordial** based on:

- 500 g pandan
- 750 g sugar
- Abasolo whisky
- Nixta corn liqueur
- pandan extract

No quantities for the Abasolo whisky, Nixta corn liqueur or pandan extract were reproduced in this chat.

The research explicitly characterised this as a more elaborate preparation than the Pacific Cocktail Haven formulation.

A third professional precedent was cited from **Diageo Bar Academy**, described as a **kaffir-lime–pandan cordial** used in the Singaporean Gimlet / Temasek Tides material.

No exact formulation quantities for the Diageo pandan cordial were reproduced in this chat.

The conclusion recorded was:

“So Pandan is not a speculative inclusion anymore. It has serious cocktail precedent. What we still need to establish experimentally is what the non-alcoholic platform version should be.”

The later Round 1 planning placed Pandan in Wave B, after the simpler initial Yuzu, Strawberry–Vanilla and Peach batches.

### Mango

Mango replaced passion fruit in the Round 1 candidate set specifically because an existing successful Mango Matcha already uses purée.

The research reframed Mango as a controlled challenge to the central thesis:

“Can a mango cordial replace mango purée without making an already excellent Mango Matcha worse — while becoming more versatile and operationally useful?”

The research explicitly recognised the advantages of purée:

“Purée has some advantages we shouldn't design away accidentally. It gives mango matcha body, opacity, ripe-fruit intensity and texture. A clarified or highly acidified bartender-style cordial might have better shelf life and cocktail performance but produce an inferior matcha. If so, it fails our central proposition.”

The professional recipe hunt then identified a formulation attributed to **Nathalie Durrieu / Experimental Cocktail Club**, published by StarChefs and associated with *Mezcal y Soda*.

It was described as:

“By far the most interesting professional formulation found: clarified mango, acids, salt and glycerin. It gives us a genuine alternative to purée rather than mango-flavoured sugar syrup.”

The recorded formulation was:

- 6 kg frozen mango
- enzymatic/fining clarification
- then, for each litre of clarified mango:
  - 500 g sugar
  - 20 g tartaric acid
  - 12 g citric acid
  - 8 g salt
  - 8 g vegetable glycerin

The research stated that the cordial is used at:

- 60 ml

in a mezcal highball.

No exact enzyme or fining-agent identities, amounts or detailed clarification method were reproduced in the chat.

The interpretation recorded was:

“That gives us exactly the experiment we wanted: one professional technique deliberately removes mango's solids while trying to preserve fruit character and rebuild texture with glycerin.”

The major caveat was then stated:

“But that also exposes the central risk: Huong's purée may be better precisely because it isn't clarified. Mango Matcha probably benefits from pulp, body and ripe-fruit density. So for Mango I would not immediately declare the ECC recipe our winner. It becomes Mango Batch 001-Cordial, tested directly against Mango Control-Purée.”

The proposed development structure was:

- A — current purée control
- B — exact professional cordial reference
- C — adapted platform cordial, only after B indicates what needs fixing

The research insisted that the cordial was not permitted to “win” merely because it was operationally convenient and stated:

“The cordial version must score at least as well as the purée control in the matcha. Being cheaper, tidier or more versatile isn't enough if the bestseller becomes less delicious.”

Mango was subsequently placed in Wave B of laboratory development because it represented a more demanding thesis test and required clarification materials and a controlled sensory comparison.

### Comparative assessment / candidate selection

The original top-five crossover ranking before Mango and Pandan were fully incorporated was:

| Cordial | Matcha evidence | Professional cocktail evidence | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Yuzu | ★★★★★ | ★★★★★ | #1 — essential |
| Strawberry | ★★★★★ | ★★★★★ | #2 — commercial workhorse |
| Peach | ★★★★☆ | ★★★★★ | #3 — elegant / accessible |
| Lychee | ★★★★☆ | ★★★★★ | #4 — very Le La Hanoi |
| Lavender-citrus | ★★★★★ | ★★★★☆ | #5 — excellent, but dose carefully |
| Passion fruit | ★★★★☆ | ★★★★☆ | Worth developing later |
| Elderflower | ★★★☆☆ | ★★★★★ | Better cocktail than matcha crossover |

After examination of the actual professional formulations, the research changed the proposed development order to:

“Yuzu → Peach → Strawberry-Vanilla → Lychee → Lavender-Grapefruit.”

The stated reason for moving Peach upward was the Diageo formulation and its combination of acid architecture, salt, fruit, tea, herbs and spice.

The candidate set subsequently evolved. Passion fruit was dropped in favour of Mango because Mango Matcha already had a successful purée-based incumbent. Pandan was selected as a sixth archetype.

The final Round 1 set in this chat became:

1. Yuzu — aromatic citrus / acid.
2. Strawberry–Vanilla — berry / dairy-friendly / mass appeal.
3. Peach–Tea–Thyme — multi-component culinary cordial.
4. Lychee — floral / saline / acid-adjusted.
5. Pandan — aromatic/non-Western flavour direction.
6. Mango — purée replacement challenge.

The strongest Batch 001 references later nominated were:

| Candidate | Selected reference | Recorded rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Yuzu | Miles Macquarrie / Kimball House | Exact formulation, credible bartender, clear acid architecture, reproducible |
| Strawberry–Vanilla | Employees Only / *Speakeasy* | Exact published recipe, strong professional provenance, vanilla bridge to matcha |
| Peach–Tea–Thyme | Diageo Bar Academy | Exact formulation, multi-acid base, sous-vide, tea/herb complexity |
| Lychee | Adam Fournier / Spago Beverly Hills | Exact salted, acidified formulation; alcoholic limitation |
| Pandan | Kevin Diedrich / Pacific Cocktail Haven | Exact formulation, strong provenance, simple extraction; alcoholic limitation |
| Mango | Nathalie Durrieu / Experimental Cocktail Club | Clarified mango with acids, salt and glycerin; genuine structural alternative to purée |

The subsequent experimental recommendation was to split Round 1 into two waves.

Wave A — establish the laboratory method:

- Yuzu Batch 001 — Kimball House
- Peach–Tea–Thyme Batch 001 — Diageo Bar Academy
- Strawberry–Vanilla Batch 001 — Employees Only

Wave B — challenge the thesis:

- Mango cordial vs existing mango purée
- Pandan
- Lychee

The research explicitly established the principle:

“No adaptations in Batch 001 unless the professional recipe is physically impossible to reproduce with available ingredients/equipment. Batch 001 teaches us what the source actually created. Batch 002 is where we earn the right to meddle.”

The broader selection criterion developed in this chat was that a cordial should function as a **platform ingredient** rather than simply be pleasant. Proposed evaluation dimensions included flavour quality, versatility, prep burden, likely utilisation and waste reduction.

The research also warned against forcing every cordial across every category. A candidate might succeed across three categories while being inappropriate for coffee, for example.

For Mango specifically, sensory performance against the purée control was separated from operational performance. The proposed sensory dimensions were:

- mango intensity
- texture/body
- sweetness
- acidity
- matcha integration
- aroma
- overall preference

Operational dimensions proposed separately included:

- prep time
- cost
- shelf life
- number of credible applications
- waste

- **The original research was generated through web research but was not a formal source-validation pass.** URLs and recipe details should be checked against the original source before migration into validated `02_sources/` or `03_professional_recipes/` records.

- **Kimball House Yuzu:** the earlier response converted the recipe into a ~500 ml test batch and introduced “lemon peel — or yuzu peel if available.” That peel substitution was not established as source-stated and must not be treated as part of the professional recipe.

- **Kimball House Yuzu:** the research called the source formulation an “exact formulation,” but this export cannot establish from the historical record alone whether the method paraphrase, peel description and approximate 1 L yield exactly match the publication. Validate before assigning exact-recipe status locally.

- **Employees Only:** metric values such as 1.81 kg strawberries, 200 g sugar and 240 ml water were conversions of the recorded imperial/cup formulation. The quarter-batch quantities were calculations and are not source-stated recipes.

- **Employees Only classification ambiguity:** the earlier research itself noted that the preparation is technically described as somewhere between a purée and syrup rather than a modern acidified cordial. It was nevertheless selected as the Strawberry–Vanilla Round 1 reference.

- **Brian Elder:** the research makes the important claim that the preparation was developed to reduce prep time and spent-fruit waste. This is highly relevant to the project thesis, but the exact wording and strength of that claim should be checked against the Liquor.com source before quoting it in publication.

- **Diageo Peach:** the one-month refrigerated shelf-life statement is a professional source claim. It was not independently validated as a food-safety claim in this chat. It must remain operational/source-stated until the food-science stream validates its interpretation.

- **Diageo Peach:** comments about malic acid supplying lingering stone-fruit acidity and tartaric acid supplying wine-like sharpness were explanatory assistant commentary and should not be mistaken for wording from Diageo.

- **The Sylvester / StarChefs:** the formulation is incomplete in this chat. Only the fruit combination, 40 Brix target and acid types were preserved. It must not be reconstructed from memory.

- **Spago Lychee:** US volume measures were only partly metricised. ¾ oz was converted to approximately 22 ml; this is CALCULATED. Purée cups, tablespoons and teaspoons were not converted to grams.

- **Spago Lychee:** because the recipe includes Giffard Lychi-Li, it is alcoholic and cannot serve unchanged as a non-alcoholic café/matcha cordial.

- **Birds of Paradise Elote-Pandan:** only part of the ingredient list and two exact quantities were preserved. It is not a complete recipe.
