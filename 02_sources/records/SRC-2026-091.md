## Extracted claims

ATAGO states that Brix by itself indicates the overall amount of dissolved solids in a solution. Its guidance for a handheld model designed for milky samples says that opaque, inhomogeneous liquids can make the boundary line difficult to read with a conventional refractometer and identifies cream sauces, yoghurt, mayonnaise and emulsions as challenging matrices. ATAGO also publishes water calibration and temperature-controlled specifications for its refractometers.

## Project interpretation

- Clear sugar syrups and filtered cordials can be recorded as measured Brix when the instrument produces a readable boundary and its manual is followed.
- Pumpkin/fruit purées and nut/seed/dairy emulsions are reported as **apparent Brix** or **apparent refractometer reading** because non-sugar dissolved solids and optical/matrix effects contribute.
- A reading from the ordered Proster device is not assigned ATAGO's model-specific accuracy. The Proster manual and repeatability observed on receipt control that device.
- Equal readings do not prove equal sweetness, sugar content, water activity, safety or shelf life.
